Audit: Federal Title & Custody / PWAP Murals (KY)

Scope & Limits

This artifact audits the legal title and custodial status of artwork produced under the Public Works of Art Project (1933–34), specifically site-specific frescoes versus movable easel works. It incorporates administrative directives from 1933 and jurisprudence through 2025.

  • Includes: PWAP "Bulletin No. 1," Berry v. UK (2020), Foster v. US (2025).
  • Excludes: Moral adjudication of content; copyright status beyond title; aesthetic critique.
  • Ends: At the dismissal of Berry and the Foster ruling.

Record

PWAP "Bulletin No. 1" (Para 1) mandates that works executed for a specific place in a public building be "treated as a part of that building." [2]
Uncontested
PWAP "Bulletin No. 1" (Para 3) states movable works are "property of the Federal Government." [2]
Uncontested
PWAP artists were paid weekly wages by the Civil Works Administration (CWA), not purchase prices. [3] [6]
Uncontested
Foster v. United States (2025) ruled that the Federal Government does not abandon title to New Deal art through neglect or passage of time. [8]
Uncontested
Berry v. University of Kentucky (2020) established a finding of fact that removing a fresco constitutes its destruction. [10]
Judicial Fact
The removal of the O'Hanlon mural renders Karyn Olivier's "Witness" (2018) "fractured" or "mute." [10]
Contextual

Conditions of Production & Use

  • Origin: Produced 1933–34 by U.S. Treasury Dept (PWAP) funded by CWA.
  • Purpose: Dual mandate: (1) Economic relief for artists (wage labor); (2) Creation of an "American Scene" historical record.
  • Constraint: "Bulletin No. 1" acted as the governing protocol, bifurcating title based on physical adhesion (fixture vs. chattel).
  • Function: Intended as permanent public infrastructure, distinct from private commodity art.

Visibility & Shrouding

  • Visible: The physical image (O'Hanlon fresco); the responsive installation (Olivier).
  • Partially Visible: The administrative distinction between "allocation" (custody) and "gift" (title transfer).
  • Shrouded: Specific inventory receipts for many allocated works; congressional acts authorizing divestment (none exist).
  • Projection: The viewer must project the historical "dialogue" between O'Hanlon and Olivier; without the O'Hanlon, the Olivier requires mental reconstruction of the missing interlocutor.

Analysis (Bounded)

Issue: Title to Site-Specific Fresco
Standard: Common Law Accession + PWAP Bulletin No. 1 Para 1.
Application: O'Hanlon mural was executed for UK Memorial Hall. It is "treated as part of that building." Title resides with the University (Building Owner).
Issue: Title to Movable Allocations
Standard: Federal Property Clause + Foster (2025).
Application: Allocation is custodial. Unless expressly divested by Congress, title remains with the United States. Neglect does not sever title.
Issue: Destruction via Removal
Standard: Berry Judicial Finding.
Application: Because the medium is fresco (pigment in plaster), removal is physically impossible without destroying the wall/art. Removal acts are destruction acts.

Risks & One-Way Doors

[IRREVERSIBLE] Separation of Fresco: Removing the O'Hanlon mural destroys the plaster substrate. This cannot be undone.

[IRREVERSIBLE] Fracture of Witness: Removing the O'Hanlon mural permanently alters the context of the Olivier installation, breaking the site-specific contract.

[REVERSIBLE] Covering: Temporary shrouding or boarding over the mural preserves the physical object and title chain.

Formal Refusals

I would rather not conclude that "allocation" constitutes a transfer of title without evidence of a Congressional act of divestment.
I would rather not treat the O'Hanlon mural as an independent object separate from the Memorial Hall realty, given the fresco medium.
I would rather not interpret the removal of the mural as a purely administrative relocation, given the judicial finding that removal equals destruction.
