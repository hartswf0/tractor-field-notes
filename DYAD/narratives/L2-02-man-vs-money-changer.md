# Round 2, Match 2 (L2-02)
## THE MAN meets THE MONEY-CHANGER
## The Auction of the Real

**Real Authors**: Michel Serres × Michel Serres

---

[NARRATOR]
The gavel hangs in the air, suspended by a silence so thick it feels like velvet. We are in the final room of the Auction House at the End of History. The walls are lined with screens displaying fluctuating currencies—crypto, carbon credits, attention futures—but in the center of the room, on a simple wooden pedestal, sits a stone.

It is a rough, heavy stone, perhaps a pestle, perhaps a weapon. It is the last Object.

Two figures approach the podium. One is solid, heavy-set, his hands calloused as if he has spent centuries shaping matter. The other is fluid, wearing a coat of many colors that seems to shift pattern when you look away—a harlequin, a joker, a man made of transactions.

[SPEAKER_A: THE MAN]
The Man reaches out and touches the stone. His hand rests on it with a familiarity that spans millennia.

"This," he says, his voice like grinding tectonic plates, "is where it begins. And this is where it stays. You think society is built on words? On contracts? On the breath between people? No. It is built on *this*. The object. The stabilizer. We hated each other, we killed each other, until we found the stone that stood between us. The object absorbs our violence. It anchors the drift."

He looks at the screens with disdain.

"You want to turn this into a number. You want to make it flow. But if you melt the anchor, the ship drifts. And we are all on the ship."

[SPEAKER_B: THE MONEY-CHANGER]
The Money-Changer laughs, a sound like coins cascading into a metal tray. He dances around the pedestal, never touching the stone, only gesturing at it.

"My dear friend, you are so heavy! You carry the weight of the world on your back. But look—" he points to the screens "—the world has already dissolved. The stone is not an anchor; it is a *token*. Its value is not in its weight, but in what it can be exchanged for. I am the Joker. I am the blank tile that can complete any sequence. I am the translation of your heavy stone into bread, into power, into flight."

He leans in, his eyes gleaming.

"Why be a stone when you can be the wind? The stone sits in one place. The coin travels everywhere. I am the universal visitor. I pass through walls because I have no shape of my own."

[SPEAKER_A: THE MAN]
"You have no shape," The Man agrees, "and therefore you have no memory. You are the white noise of history. You say you are the universal visitor, but you are the universal solvent. You dissolve the bonds that hold us together. When I give a gift, the object carries the trace of my hand. It binds me to the receiver. When you pay with money, the transaction is finished. The bond is severed. You create a society of strangers who owe each other nothing."

He grips the stone tighter.

"The object is the excluded third that makes the collective possible. We gather *around* the fire, *around* the table, *around* the statue. Take away the object, and we are just a mob facing each other in the dark."

[SPEAKER_B: THE MONEY-CHANGER]
"A mob?" The Money-Changer spins, his coat flashing gold and silver. "No, a network! A market! A vast, breathing brain of exchange! You want to freeze us around your statues, paralyzed by reverence. I want to set us free. Money is the degree of freedom of the system. It allows the baker to buy shoes without finding a cobbler who wants bread. It is the miracle of the excluded middle! I am not the solvent; I am the lubricant. Without me, your heavy gears would grind to a halt."

He pulls a coin from the air—or perhaps it was always in his hand.

"This coin has the face of Caesar, or the President, or the Algorithm. It doesn't matter. It is a mask. And because it is a mask, it allows us to play. You want us to be earnest. I want us to be *possible*."

[NARRATOR]
The screens flicker. The price of the stone is rising—not in dollars, but in *possibility*. The Man looks at the stone, then at the Money-Changer. He realizes that to save the object, he might have to buy it. And to buy it, he would have to become a customer.

[SPEAKER_A: THE MAN]
"You offer possibility," The Man says slowly, "but at the price of reality. You turn the world into a casino where the house always wins. And who is the house? It is the one who holds the Joker. You say you are the lubricant, but you are the parasite. You take a cut of every exchange. You grow fat on the friction of our lives."

He lifts the stone. It is heavy, undeniably real.

"I will not bid. I will not exchange. I will simply... hold. The object resists. That is its nature. It resists your alchemy. You can put a price on it, but you cannot eat the price. When the lights go out, when the screens die, the stone will still be here. And your coins will be just metal discs with dead men's faces."

[SPEAKER_B: THE MONEY-CHANGER]
The Money-Changer stops dancing. For a moment, he looks almost sad.

"When the lights go out," he whispers, "you will be alone in the dark with your stone. And you will realize that the stone has no meaning unless there is someone else to see it. And to bring that someone else to you... you will need me. I am not the parasite, brother. I am the relation itself. I am the space *between* you and the other. You hold the stone, but I hold the space."

[SHIFT]
The Man looks at the stone, then at the vast, empty room. He feels the weight of it—the terrible, isolating weight of the real.

"Then we are both damned," The Man says. "I am the anchor that drowns the swimmer. And you are the water that dissolves the land."

"No," says the Money-Changer, offering his hand—which is empty, and therefore ready to receive. "We are not damned. We are the system. You provide the nodes. I provide the edges. Shall we dance?"

[NARRATOR]
The Man hesitates. Then, with a sigh that sounds like a foundation settling, he places the stone in the Money-Changer's hand. It does not turn into gold. It simply... circulates.
