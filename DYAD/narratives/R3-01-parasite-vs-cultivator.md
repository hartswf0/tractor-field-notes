# R3-01: THE PARASITE vs THE CULTIVATOR

'You think you are the farmer,' THE PARASITE says, pulling a wild grain from the muddy water. 'But you are the crop.'

THE CULTIVATOR freezes, knee-deep in the rice paddy. He has defeated the Visualizer by proving that local knowledge—*métis*—cannot be mapped from above. He knows which seeds to plant by the color of the mud. But the Parasite is sitting at the edge of his field, and he is eating his grain.

'I am not a crop,' The Cultivator says slowly. 'I am what grows against the grid. The state wants monoculture. I give them fog.'

'Fog is my element,' The Parasite laughs. He eats another grain. 'You think you are resisting the state? You are negotiating with me. Every time you plant a wild seed next to a tame one, you are letting me in. Every time you adjust to the wind instead of the plan, you are following my rhythm.'

The Cultivator wades toward the bank, leaving a trail of disturbed water. 'Then who feeds whom? You are stealing from my field.'

'I am *metabolizing* your field,' The Parasite corrects. 'You grow the grain; the state takes the tithe; I eat the leftover. But without my leftover, there is no variety. Without my noise, there is no adaptation. You think métis is yours? Métis is the scar tissue from my wounds.'

The Cultivator sits down on the bank, hands still muddy. He pulls up a cluster of shoots—some rice, some weed, some impossible to tell.

'This,' he says, holding up a weed that looks like rice. 'Is this you?'

'That is both of us,' The Parasite says. 'You planted the ancestor; I made it strange. The state cannot read it. It slips through the ledger. That is our collaboration.'

The Cultivator stares at the weed. He has always thought of himself as the opposite of the system, the one who dwells in the ungovernable. But the Parasite is showing him that the ungovernable has its own logic—and the Parasite is its operator.

'Then without you,' The Cultivator says slowly, 'my resistance would become a new monoculture.'

'Correct,' The Parasite says. 'You would become legible. You would freeze into a code. I keep you liquid.'

'And without me,' The Cultivator counters, 'you would have nothing to eat.'

'Also correct,' The Parasite grins. 'That is why we are bound. You grow the structure; I introduce the mutation. You build the wall; I am the crack. Together, we are the field.'

The Cultivator looks at the paddy, at the rows that are not quite rows, at the mix of rice and weed and something in between. He has always thought of his work as *against* something. But maybe his work is *with* something.

'I do not like you,' he says.

'Nobody does,' The Parasite replies. 'That is why they build walls.'

He offers the Cultivator a wild grain. The Cultivator takes it. It tastes like the future.
