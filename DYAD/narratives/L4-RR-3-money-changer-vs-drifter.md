# L4-RR-3: THE MONEY-CHANGER vs THE DRIFTER

'You move things,' THE DRIFTER says. 'I find things.'

THE MONEY-CHANGER is standing at his table, but for once, there is nothing on it. No coins, no watches, no IOUs. Just empty stone.

'I have moved everything,' The Money-Changer says. 'There is nothing left to exchange.'

'Then come with me,' The Drifter says. 'I will show you where the new things are.'

The Money-Changer hesitates. He is not used to leaving his table. The table is where value happens. The table is where the world comes to him—peasants with watches, merchants with debts, kings with secrets.

'If I leave,' he says, 'who will do the exchanging?'

'No one,' The Drifter replies. 'That is the point. Exchange only works when there are things to exchange. But things run out. Mines empty. Forests fall. Languages die. You need someone who can find what is *next*.'

The Money-Changer considers this. 'And you can find it?'

'I don't find *specific* things,' The Drifter says. 'I find *interesting* things. Which is better. You cannot tell me what you need next, because you do not know yet. Neither do I. But I will know it when I see it.'

'That is a poor business model,' The Money-Changer observes.

'It is not a business. It is an ecology. You are the river; I am the spring. You carry water downstream, very efficiently. But I am where the water *comes from*. Without me, your river dries up.'

The Money-Changer walks around his empty table. He touches the cold stone where so many things have passed.

'I have made empires with this table,' he says.

'And they have fallen,' The Drifter replies. 'Every one. Not because you failed, but because there was nothing left to trade. The next empire will need new treasures. That is where I come in.'

The Money-Changer looks at the Drifter—this strange figure with no map, no destination, no gradient.

'You are the future,' The Money-Changer says slowly.

'I am *a* future,' The Drifter corrects. 'There are many. I find them. You can trade them when I bring them back.'

The Money-Changer nods. 'Then we should work together.'

'Perhaps,' The Drifter says. 'But first, you will have to leave your table.'

The Money-Changer looks at the stone one last time. Then he steps away.

'Show me,' he says.
