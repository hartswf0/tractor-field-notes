## 1. Calibration & Rubric Digest

**Core Task**: Adjudicate the final clash between two Geertzian operators—THE ANTHROPOLOGIST (witness/observer) vs THE ARCHITECT (constructor/builder).

**Evaluation Frame**: Ontological priority. Which mode of thick description has causal primacy?

**Darkness Prior**: MAXIMUM. Both operators derive from the same theoretical source (Geertz, 1973). Risk of false distinction is high. The Guardian must determine whether this is a genuine competition or an internal dialogue.

**Format**: Best of Three  
**Settings**: Village → Archive → Ruins  
**Scoring**: Each match assessed on 100-point scale per candidate.

## 5. Final Verdict

The aggregate scoring favors THE ANTHROPOLOGIST by 2 points. However, the Guardian notes that this margin is within statistical noise and that Match 3 produced a paradox: both candidates reversed their positions.

**The Guardian's ruling is as follows**:

### PRIMARY VERDICT
**THE ANTHROPOLOGIST** wins the Championship (286 vs 284).

### SECONDARY RULING
The victory is **pyrrhic**. By winning Match 3 through construction (not observation), The Anthropologist has demonstrated that his method *requires* the Architect's method to function in conditions of total opacity. Thick description without access to living informants collapses into thick construction.

### TERTIARY OBSERVATION
The Architect's confession—"I am you"—is not a concession but a **diagnosis**. The tournament revealed that the two operators are not alternatives but *phases* of the same process:

1. **Phase 1 (Anthropologist)**: Long-term observation in living contexts.
2. **Phase 2 (Architect)**: Structural construction in dead/opaque contexts.

Both are required. Neither is sufficient alone.
