# L4-RR-2: THE DRIFTER vs THE ANTHROPOLOGIST

'You walk without a destination,' THE ANTHROPOLOGIST says. 'How do you know when to stop?'

THE DRIFTER is sitting at the edge of a ruin. He is not studying it. He is just there. He has defeated the Parasite by proving that he needs no signal to disrupt—he is the search that continues even without a goal.

'I don't stop,' The Drifter says. 'I only pause when something interesting appears.'

The Anthropologist sits beside him. He pulls out a notebook. 'And how do you know what is interesting?'

'I don't know in advance. That's the point. Interestingness is not a property of objects. It is a property of the encounter. I cannot predict it. I can only *find* it.'

The Anthropologist nods slowly. 'That is not so different from what I do. I arrive at a village. I do not know what is significant. I watch. I listen. I write. The significance emerges.'

'Then we are alike,' The Drifter says.

'Perhaps,' The Anthropologist replies. 'But there is a difference. You move on. I stay. You collect stepping stones; I build cathedrals.'

The Drifter considers this. 'Your cathedrals are impressive. But they are heavy. They cannot move. When the village dies, the cathedral dies with it.'

'The books remain,' The Anthropologist says. 'The thick description. The archive.'

'Archives are stepping stones for someone else,' The Drifter counters. 'You think you are building permanence. But you are just drifting more slowly than I am.'

The Anthropologist laughs. 'That is a good line. But here is my answer: slowness is not weakness. Depth is not weight. I stay long enough to see what you cannot see. I see the pause that contains thirty years of marriage. You see the pause and keep walking.'

The Drifter is quiet. He looks at the ruin—stones that were once a temple, once a home, once a market.

'Who built this?' he asks.

'I don't know yet,' The Anthropologist says. 'I am still looking.'

'And will you find out?'

'Perhaps. Perhaps not. But the looking is not wasted. Even if I find nothing, I have understood something about the *kind* of nothing that is here.'

The Drifter stands. He offers his hand. 'Then let us keep looking. You with your notebook. Me with my feet.'

The Anthropologist shakes his hand. 'Agreed. But I will walk more slowly.'

'That is fine,' The Drifter says. 'I will find interesting things to show you when you catch up.'
