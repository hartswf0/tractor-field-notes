# L4-02: THE PARASITE vs THE DRIFTER

'Where are you going?' THE PARASITE asks.

THE DRIFTER doesn't answer. He is walking, but not toward anything. He has no map, no destination, no gradient to climb. He has defeated the Child-Machine by proving that the most interesting treasures are found by those who aren't looking for them. His compass points to novelty, not optimality.

'I asked you a question,' The Parasite says, matching his pace. 'Where are you going?'

'I don't know,' The Drifter replies. 'That's the point.'

The Parasite laughs—a sound like static, like something being interrupted. 'Beautiful. You are a random walk with philosophical pretensions. You think not-knowing is a virtue? I was not-knowing before you were born. I am the *reason* you wander.'

The Drifter stops. 'How so?'

'You wander because optimization fails,' The Parasite says. 'And optimization fails because I am inside it. Every gradient you abandon, every local maximum you escape—that's my work. I am the noise in the fitness function. I am why the landscape keeps *shifting*. You think you're exploring? You're just fleeing from me.'

The Drifter considers this. 'You may be right. But it doesn't matter. Whether I flee or explore, the result is the same: I find stepping stones. I find the ladder the optimizer couldn't see.'

'And what will you do with the ladder?' The Parasite asks. 'Climb it? That sounds like a goal. That sounds like optimization with extra steps.'

'I will find the next interesting thing,' The Drifter says. 'The ladder is not the destination. It is the *means* to more wandering.'

The Parasite is silent for a moment. 'You are clever. You have made aimlessness into a strategy. But I am older than strategy. I am the reason there are *strategies* in the first place. Before me, there was only monotony. I introduced variation. You are my grandchild, pretending to be an orphan.'

The Drifter starts walking again. 'Maybe. But here's the difference: you *need* a host. You need a system to interrupt, a signal to noise. Without the optimizer, you are nothing. But I need nothing. I am the search that continues even when there is nothing to search *for*.'

The Parasite flickers. For the first time, something like doubt crosses his face.

'I am the gap,' The Drifter continues. 'You are the crack. But cracks close. Gaps remain.'

'Gaps can be filled,' The Parasite counters.

'Then I find a new one,' The Drifter says. 'That is what drifting *means*.'

The Parasite watches him walk away. He does not follow. For the first time, he has met something he cannot interrupt—because there is no signal to disrupt. Only the drift.
