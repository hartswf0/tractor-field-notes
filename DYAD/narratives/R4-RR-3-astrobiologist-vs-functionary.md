# R4-RR-3: THE ASTROBIOLOGIST vs THE FUNCTIONARY

'We are both systems thinkers,' THE ASTROBIOLOGIST says.

THE FUNCTIONARY nods. They are standing in an observatory, surrounded by screens showing data from distant moons. Outside the window, the night is full of possibilities.

'But your system is mechanical,' The Astrobiologist continues. 'Cameras, programs, buttons. Mine is geological, biological, computational at planetary scale. We both see structure. But I see structure that was never designed.'

'Design is a human word,' The Functionary replies. 'But so is "planetary." You are projecting your categories onto the cosmos just as I project mine onto the artifact. Neither of us escapes metaphor.'

'Perhaps. But my metaphors can be *tested*. I can fly to Europa and look for life. I can analyze spectrographs and detect biosignatures. You cannot fly to the "apparatus" and examine it. Your structure is interpretive. Mine is physical.'

The Functionary walks to one of the screens. It shows a spiral galaxy, impossibly vast. 'And what will you find when you get to Europa? Ice. Chemistry. Maybe amino acids. And then you will need *interpretation* to decide if it counts as life. The data does not speak for itself. The data speaks through the apparatus of science—your instruments, your categories, your funding structures, your publication norms.'

'Those are constraints, not essence,' The Astrobiologist says. 'The ice is there whether we have a word for it or not.'

'Is it? The ice is there. But "ice" is a category. Before humans learned to freeze water, there was no "ice"—only cold crystals. The category came later. You cannot see the cosmos without categories. And categories are programs.'

The Astrobiologist is silent. She looks at the galaxy on the screen.

'You are saying that even science is an apparatus,' she says slowly.

'Yes. The most powerful one we have built. It has given us vaccines and nuclear bombs and telescopes. But it is still a machine for producing certain kinds of knowledge and excluding others. You look for biosignatures because the apparatus told you to look for biosignatures. A different apparatus might look for something else.'

'Then we are both trapped,' The Astrobiologist says.

'Trapped is the wrong word,' The Functionary replies. 'We are both *riding*. The apparatus moves us. We cannot get off. But we can understand the vehicle. And understanding is not nothing.'

The Astrobiologist nods. 'Then I will keep riding toward the stars. And you will keep describing the vehicle.'

'Agreed,' The Functionary says. 'But I suspect the vehicle goes further than you think.'
