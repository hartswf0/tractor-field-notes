# R4-02: THE FUNCTIONARY vs THE PHILOSOPHER

'I see you have a camera,' THE FUNCTIONARY says.

THE PHILOSOPHER does not answer. She is holding the device, but she is not taking pictures. She is looking at it—this box of glass and metal, this apparatus that shapes what can be seen.

'You use it to critique,' The Functionary continues. 'You write books about the danger of images, the violence of framing, the anesthesia of spectacle. Very admirable. But have you ever asked *who taught you to critique*?'

'I taught myself,' The Philosopher says. 'Through careful looking.'

The Functionary shakes his head. 'No. The apparatus taught you. The camera created the category of "image" that you now critique. Before photography, there was no "image problem." You are critiquing a world that the camera made possible. Your rebellion is within the program.'

The Philosopher sets down the camera. 'That is clever. You are saying that even my resistance is encoded. That the apparatus contains its own critique as one of its functions.'

'Precisely,' The Functionary says. 'You are a button. A very sophisticated button—one that produces essays instead of photographs—but a button nonetheless. You think you are free because you can say "no" to the image. But the program *includes* the "no." It counts on the "no." The critic is just another operator.'

'And what are you?' The Philosopher asks.

'I am the one who *sees* the program. I do not critique it; I *describe* it. I show you the walls you did not know existed. And then—here is the important part—I invite you to *play* within them, knowingly. Not to escape, because there is no escape. But to play with awareness.'

The Philosopher picks up the camera again. 'So my grief is part of the program. My tears for the dead boy in the photograph—that too is a function.'

'Yes,' The Functionary says. 'But that does not make it less real. The grief is real. The tears are real. They are just *also* generated by the apparatus. Knowing this does not erase the feeling. It deepens it.'

'How so?'

'Because now you are grieving *and* understanding. You are inside the system *and* seeing it. That is the only freedom available: not to escape, but to be conscious. To photograph and know that you are photographing. To cry and know that you are performing the cry.'

The Philosopher looks through the viewfinder. She sees the Functionary framed in the center, surrounded by the edges of the glass.

'You are asking me to be ironic,' she says.

'I am asking you to be *complete*,' The Functionary replies. 'The critique is not the exit. The exit does not exist. But consciousness is not nothing.'

She takes the picture.
