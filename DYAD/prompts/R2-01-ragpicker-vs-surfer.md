'We ants', he declares, 'are not isolated individuals. Our brains may be no
bigger than pin heads, yet we can achieve great things. We can accomplish 
these feats because we collaborate. In a word, we are the most social of insects'.

SPIDER, more solitary by nature, finds the idea of life in a colony hard to
grasp. 'In the course of your activities', she remarks, 'you have to deal
with all sorts of things. Tell me, do you have social relations with these 
things or only with mature members of the colony like yourself?'

'Absolutely', ANT continues. 'Anything can belong to the network, whether ant
or non-ant. I insist that these things are not just passive objects. I am bound 
up in relations with them, as I am with my fellow ants. And they are caught up 
in it just as flies, my dear spider, are caught up in your web'.

'But there you are surely wrong', exclaims SPIDER. 'The lines of my web are
not at all like those of your network. In your world there are just entities 
that are assembled so as to make things happen. Every "relation" in the network 
is a connection between one entity and another. The lines of my web, to the 
contrary, are themselves spun from materials exuded from my own body.'
